# Phase 12 Design Counter-Review

1. **BLOCKER — the open crash order can delete a valid human decision.** Chunk 4 writes `request.json`, removes `gate.decision`, publishes `gate.json`, and only then commits `state.open_gate`; the required crash cut between `gate.json` and state therefore exposes a fillable live interface before durable state names it. A human/helper can validly write `gate.decision` in that window, after which retry follows the no-`open_gate` open path and deletes the decision as stale. That loses an explicit human act and violates REQ-22, REQ-36, and REQ-37. **Suggested resolution:** either commit the open state before exposing `gate.json` and reconstruct the interface on recovery, or bind any existing `gate.decision` to the archived request and preserve/resolve it when it matches, removing only bytes proven to belong to another or already-closed gate. Add a crash fixture that writes a valid decision after interface publication but before the state commit.

2. **BLOCKER — resolution can archive stale decision bytes captured before lock reacquisition.** `waitForGateInterface` returns raw `{kind:"interface", bytes}`, and chunk 6 says to reacquire the task lock, reread only `request.json`, and discriminate those captured bytes. Because `gate.decision` is a replaceable atomic interface, the human can correct or cancel it after a waiter reads it but before that waiter acquires the lock; two waiters can likewise carry different generations. The service can then commit an approval that is no longer the current explicit decision. **Suggested resolution:** under the resolve lock, reread and canonically validate the current `gate.decision` and archive exactly that observation; alternatively compare a captured digest with the locked reread and return to waiting if it changed. Cover approval-to-cancel and cancel-to-approval replacement while lock acquisition is delayed.

3. **BLOCKER — changed reuse of an `intent_id` is not discoverable through the lookup described in chunk 4.** The proposed `gate_id` includes `request_digest`, so changed inputs derive a different `decisions/<gate-id>/` directory. Reading only the newly derived request/decision paths cannot find the older request whose `intent_id` matches and digest differs. While the old gate is live, the current ordering instead reports `GATE_ACTIVE`; after cancellation, supersession, or another closure without a successful receipt, it can open a second gate. Both miss REQ-23's required `INTENT_MISMATCH`. **Suggested resolution:** pin a stable intent-to-gate lookup (or bounded archive lookup) and inspect the current `open_gate` request before classifying another gate as merely active. Test changed reuse against both a live prior gate and a resolved non-success prior gate. Do not change the architecture-pinned gate-ID formula silently.

4. **BLOCKER — the supplemental ledger promotes unauthenticated interface/caller claims into closure authority.** The design calls `gate.json` mutable and non-authoritative, excludes `supplemental_outcome` from the request digest, then copies the ledger from `gate.json` into immutable `decision.json` and uses it to suppress re-raising. Chunks 5–6 never require loading and validating the referenced gate-counter review and triage artifacts; a fabricated `SupplementalReviewOutcome` or edited `gate.json` can therefore mark evidence ingested, triaged, or declined and permit resolution without REQ-41's digest-bound review and triage. **Suggested resolution:** define the local projection/read seam and authenticate every ledger transition against the actual canonical review/triage bytes and their task, gate, phase, subject, input, evidence, and triage bindings. Re-derive or revalidate the ledger under the resolve lock rather than trusting `gate.json`; ensure an explicit decline is durably attributable to the human decision channel.

5. **BLOCKER — chunk 6 has no decision-effect/state matrix and literally records non-approvals as `ApprovalRef`s.** It says a decision envelope is archived and state commits while appending an `ApprovalRef` or `WaiverRef`, but the existing `GateEffect` catalogue includes retry, redirect-waiver, redirect-upstream, and non-advancing outcomes. `revise`, `reject`, `abort`, `waiver-requested`, and similar outcomes must not become approvals; cancellation returns `GATE_CANCELLED`, and supersession is not even an `archflow_gate` success payload. Implementing the stated rule corrupts durable authority and violates REQ-20/REQ-37. **Suggested resolution:** pin an exhaustive payload/effect matrix covering state mutation, result/error, receipt eligibility, replay, and whether an `ApprovalRef` is appended. Archive every explicit outcome, but append approval/authorization state only for outcomes that actually grant that authority; test every decision vocabulary arm.

6. **MAJOR — closure-first replay is not defined for states that cannot pass the resolve body's `open_gate` CAS.** A closure can coexist with (a) the matching live gate after a closure-before-state crash, (b) a matching committed intent after state committed but cleanup crashed, (c) later state, or (d) no live gate because the request/decision pair was created in fully manual mode. Chunk 4 says any closure re-drives resolution, while chunk 6 requires matching `state.open_gate`; chunks 4 and 8 do not define the alternate path for the other three cases. This will force recovery semantics to be invented during implementation and can mutate a newer gate or make manual decisions unimportable. **Suggested resolution:** pin the complete matrix: matching live gate finishes resolution; matching committed intent authenticates and replays; later state returns `INTENT_NOT_CURRENT` without touching its gate; a manual closure with no live gate enters only authenticated checkpoint/manual-decision adoption (or a specified open-then-resolve sequence). Cover state-commit-before-interface-removal and manual request/decision replay.

7. **MAJOR — the 250 ms acquisition deadline prevents the promised two-process result under the proposed locked work.** The separate top-level locked bodies do avoid `lock.ts`'s `AsyncLocalStorage` re-entrancy guard, but they still use a lock that gives up after 250 ms. The open body performs several durable reads/writes/fsyncs and commit-authorization validation; the resolve body may re-plan and apply capped restore payloads. A contender can therefore time out before observing the winner and return `IO_ERROR` rather than the REQ-24/VAL-10 `STATE_CONFLICT`, especially on WSL, loaded developer disks, or crash-test instrumentation. `lock.ts` is absent from the Files table. **Suggested resolution:** explicitly adapt the gate entry point or lock policy so a live bounded gate body can finish and the contender then rereads state/CAS (without stale-lock takeover and without holding the lock during the human wait). Add a deterministic test that holds the first body beyond 250 ms and proves the second call reaches the post-lock conflict/replay classification.

## Triage

Triaged 2026-07-30 against the design at `phases/phase-12-durable-gates-waivers-and-manual-decisions.md`. Five accepted in full, two accepted with a scoped resolution. No finding rejected outright.

1. **Open crash order can delete a valid human decision — ACCEPTED.** Correct, and it was a regression introduced by the previous round's fix, which asked for a blind removal of stale interface bytes. Chunk 4 now binds rather than deletes: bytes that bind to this gate's archived request are this gate's decision and resolve from there; only bytes proven to belong to another or already-closed gate are removed. The crash cut (valid decision written after `gate.json` publication, before the state commit) is added to Verification Steps as a decision-safety cut, and to Success Criteria.

2. **Resolution can archive stale bytes captured before lock reacquisition — ACCEPTED.** The resolution also simplifies the design. `GateWaitOutcome` drops its `bytes` member and becomes a pure signal; the resolve body re-reads and canonically validates the current `gate.decision` under the lock and archives exactly that observation. Canonical parsing and `GATE_DECISION_INVALID` move from the wait to resolve. Approval-to-cancel replacement during a delayed acquisition is added to the test list.

3. **Changed reuse of an `intent_id` is not discoverable — ACCEPTED IN PART.** The ordering half is a real bug and is fixed: chunk 4 now reads the request archived for the gate `open_gate` actually names, rather than the newly derived path, and returns `INTENT_MISMATCH` before `GATE_ACTIVE` when that request carries the caller's `intent_id` under a different `request_digest`. The gate-ID formula is unchanged. The second half — changed reuse after a closure with no success receipt — is recorded as an explicit documented limitation rather than fixed, because locating that request needs an intent-to-gate index nothing else in the system wants. Rationale recorded in the design: no approval is fabricated, and the fresh gate still requires its own human decision, so the residual gap is REQ-23 classification hygiene rather than a trust-boundary failure. Both cases are in the test list, the second asserted as documented behaviour.

4. **Supplemental ledger promotes unauthenticated claims into closure authority — ACCEPTED.** This contradicted the codebase's own agent-declared vs server-attested evidence model. The ledger is now derived under the resolve lock from observable artifacts rather than copied from mutable `gate.json`; `ingest` and `triage-no-change` are admitted only when their evidence digest binds a gate-counter artifact actually present for that exact gate, phase instance, and subject; `decline` is recorded explicitly as a caller assertion that fabricates no review. Where the structured source behind the rendered projection is not server-readable until Phase 15, the design is required to say so and verify only the projection's own digest binding rather than implying more. Scoped deliberately: no general evidence pipeline is introduced here.

5. **No decision-effect matrix; non-approvals recorded as `ApprovalRef`s — ACCEPTED.** The clearest defect in the reviewed draft. Chunk 6 now delegates to the existing `gateDecisionEffect` classifier instead of restating the vocabulary: `advance` alone appends an `ApprovalRef`/`WaiverRef` and earns a success receipt, while `retry`, `redirect-waiver`, `redirect-upstream`, and `non-advancing` archive their closure and clear `open_gate` without touching either set. Every explicit outcome is archived. Added as a Success Criterion and as a per-arm test obligation.

6. **Closure-first replay undefined for states that cannot pass the resolve CAS — ACCEPTED.** Chunk 4 now pins the four-case matrix: matching live gate finishes resolution; matching committed intent authenticates and replays; later state returns `INTENT_NOT_CURRENT` without touching the gate it names; a manual closure with no live gate goes only to chunk 8's authenticated adoption path.

7. **250 ms acquisition deadline versus the locked bodies — ACCEPTED IN PART.** The observation is correct but largely pre-existing rather than introduced here: `lock.ts`'s acquisition deadline already coexists with Phase 11 installing up to 25 MiB of payload under the same lock. The safety property holds regardless, since a contender that cannot acquire writes nothing; the gap is only whether it reports `STATE_CONFLICT` or a lock-acquisition failure. Verification Steps now require pinning the contender's actual classified failure rather than assuming `STATE_CONFLICT`, and the third Success Criterion is reworded to "writes nothing and receives a classified non-advancing failure". `lock.ts` is deliberately not added to the Files table, no lock policy change is made, and no stale-lock takeover is introduced. The general question of the acquisition deadline is flagged as belonging outside this phase.
